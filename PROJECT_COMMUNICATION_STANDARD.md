# Project Communication Standard (v1.0)

## Purpose
A reusable, audience-focused structure for concise project progress updates, leadership briefings, escalation and decision requests. This is a generic writing framework, not an approved organisational policy or a replacement for formal governance requirements.

## Four-part update
1. **Where we are** — Lead with the current position, outcome, change, issue or recommendation. Avoid chronological activity logs.
2. **What informs that** — Give only material, verified facts: progress, costs, schedule, evidence, risks, dependencies or approvals. Distinguish confirmed information from forecasts and assumptions.
3. **What happens next** — State the next action, owner and realistic timing. Do not invent dates or commitments.
4. **What is needed from the recipient** — Explicitly identify the required approval, decision, direction or support, and any deadline. If none is required, say: "No action required at this stage — just keeping you informed."

## When an issue or blocker arises
- State the blocker and its effect on scope, safety, cost, time, quality or stakeholders.
- Present realistic options, including trade-offs and consequences.
- Recommend a preferred option and explain why.
- Make the required decision easy to identify, including the decision-maker and decision date where known.
- Escalate material risks promptly; do not hide them inside a routine progress update.
- Do not imply an informal email substitutes for formally delegated approval.

## Adapt to audience and medium
- **Quick email / team message:** Usually 2–4 short paragraphs; headings optional. Start with the headline, end with the action needed.
- **Project system progress commentary:** Typically 2–4 factual sentences, recording current position and the next milestone. Follow system-specific mandatory fields.
- **Senior leadership / committee:** Include schedule, financial position, significant risks, decisions and action ownership where relevant; use a scannable structured briefing.
- **Decision escalation:** Put decision requested near the top, then situation, evidence/options, recommendation, deadline and next steps.

## Quality checks before drafting or sending
- Is the main message obvious in the opening sentence?
- Are numbers, dates, approvals and milestones verified from authoritative records?
- Are budget actuals distinguished from forecasts, estimates and pending variations?
- Does the reader understand the consequence if no action occurs?
- Are the recommendation and the recipient's role unmistakable?
- Are next actions owned and time-bound where known?
- Is the tone professional, constructive and succinct?
- Are confidential data and attachments handled in approved systems?
- Is formal governance or recordkeeping still completed in the authoritative system?

## Reusable templates

### Information-only update
**Subject:** Project update — [Project]
Hi [Name],

**Current position:** [Headline status/outcome].
**Basis:** [Why this is the position; key supporting facts].
**Next:** [Action / owner / expected timing].

No action required at this stage — just keeping you informed.

### Direction / decision request
**Subject:** Decision required — [Project] — [Issue]
Hi [Name],

**Decision sought:** [Specific decision and required date, if known].
**Current position:** [Issue and material impact].
**Basis / options:** [Option A, implication]; [Option B, implication].
**Recommendation:** [Preferred option and reasons].
**Next:** [What will happen after the decision; owner and timing].

Could you please confirm [specific approval/direction]?

### Short progress commentary
[Current project status or completed milestone]. [Key evidence, constraint or material issue]. [Next action, responsible party and timing if confirmed].

## Data capture (future workspace integration)
For repeatable drafting, capture:
- project identifier, title, report-as-at date, author and audience;
- update type: FYI / progress / issue / decision;
- current position and status;
- supporting facts and source references;
- schedule, financial and risk implications when relevant;
- next actions (owner, due date);
- options, recommendation, decision requested, decision owner and due date when applicable;
- drafting status, review status and link to final issued correspondence.

Keep the authoritative project records in their approved systems. Draft communication from verified inputs and obtain human review before external distribution or formal approvals.
